Ground rules

What the board actually does.

  • Debris blocks line of sight and costs double to cross. Beam and ballistic weapons need a clear line; missiles do not.
  • Artillery has a minimum range of three. Inside two hexes it cannot fire at all — closing is the entire answer to it.
  • Ridges are high ground: +1 weapon range, and shots fired up at them are 12% less likely to land.
  • Every weapon has a minimum range too. A beam rifle cannot fire at an adjacent enemy; a saber can only fire there.
  • Area weapons aim at the hex catching the most bodies, so a packed formation is missile food and a spread one starves them.

How pilots fail

Orders are issued. Compliance is not guaranteed.

  • Reckless pilots push forward against the break-off order, harder when the rival is on the field.
  • Frightened pilots refuse to close and break for cover, and fear compounds as the hull opens up.
  • Trauma and fatigue persist between chapters. Standing a pilot down clears them, and costs you that sortie.
  • Signatures charge by taking damage and fire once per sortie — the one thing you control mid-fight.

The ruleset

Every shot resolves on dice you can read before they are rolled.

  • To hit: roll 2d6 against a target number. It starts at the pilot's gunnery and takes modifiers for range bracket, whether either mech moved, cover, high ground and reactor heat. The whole stack is printed in the combat log next to every shot.
  • Range brackets, not a range number. Short is +0, medium +1, long +3. Closing a bracket is often worth more than any weapon upgrade.
  • Armour is a save, not subtraction. Light frames save on 5+, medium 4+, heavy 3+. A weapon's AP worsens that roll — a beam saber at AP-2 leaves a heavy mech saving on 5+, and nothing saves against 7+.
  • Damage lands somewhere. A second 2d6 picks the location. Arms carry weapons and take them with them; legs cost you movement; the head and centre torso end the sortie.
  • Heat degrades before it kills. WARM +1 to hit, HOT +2 and slower, CRITICAL +3, REDLINE +4 and a shutdown check on 8+ every tick. Only a full bar forces the scram outright.
  • Pilots roll their own gunnery. Steady nerve buys −1; being green costs +1; trauma and fatigue add up to +2 as a war drags on.
